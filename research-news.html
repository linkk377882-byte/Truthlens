Category 1 : Real-World Impact of Misinformation

Mayor Warns of London 'Disinformation Blizzard'

Sadiq Khan warns London faces a "dark blizzard of online disinformation" from foreign and far-right groups. Research shows false claims about crime and migration have risen sharply even while homicide rates remain comparatively low. Khan argues that unchecked misinformation is reshaping public perception, eroding trust, and making democratic debate more vulnerable to manipulation.

CDC Shooter Believed COVID Vaccine Was Harmful; Union Says Misinformation Put Staff at Risk

A Georgia man who blamed the COVID-19 vaccine for his depression and suicidal thoughts shot a police officer outside CDC headquarters in Atlanta before dying at the scene. Following the attack, a union representing CDC workers said that years of vaccine misinformation created a climate that endangered scientists, public-health staff, and institutions working to communicate evidence-based guidance.

Category 2 : AI-Generated Content and How to Identify It

Hospital Alert after Fake Doctor-Endorsed Videos

A South London hospital trust issued an alert after fake AI-generated videos falsely claimed its doctors had endorsed weight loss products. The trust said its clinicians would never promote such products and warned that the videos were part of a scam built on fabricated doctor profiles, altered audio, and synthetic visuals that mimic legitimate health communication.

Tripped Up by Misinformation? Here's a Refresher on Identifying AI

This explainer outlines how AI-generated content makes breaking-news environments harder to navigate. It recommends slowing down before sharing, checking the original source, looking for expert reporting, and using detection tools alongside common-sense verification habits. The focus is not only on identifying synthetic visuals but also on recognizing when urgency and repetition are being used to lower skepticism.

A Quick Guide to Spotting Misinformation

This guide explains how to spot and stop misinformation online. It distinguishes fake news from misinformation shared without malicious intent and recommends checking multiple sources, verifying author credentials, and understanding why certain narratives spread so easily. The broader goal is to build critical thinking, not simply to win arguments online.

Category 3 : Platform Policies & Responsibility

YouTube to Start Bringing Back Accounts of Creators Banned for Misinformation

YouTube announced that some creators banned under its earlier COVID-19 and election misinformation rules may now return, arguing that the platform's current policy landscape has changed. Critics say the decision reflects a broader shift in how platforms balance speech claims, moderation legitimacy, and long-term responsibility for the information ecosystems they helped shape.

As Meta Drops Fact-Checking, Critics Fear a Misinformation Spike

Critics argue that reducing formal fact-checking while AI-generated summaries become more common could create the conditions for sharper misinformation spikes during breaking news. The concern is not just that individual falsehoods will spread faster, but that platforms may increasingly optimize for engagement while giving users fewer signals about what has or has not been independently verified.

Category 4 : Misinformation During Breaking News

AI Fuels False Claims after Charlie Kirk's Death, CBS News Analysis Reveals

After the killing of activist Charlie Kirk, AI-powered misinformation spread rapidly across social media. CBS reported that chatbot answers, altered visuals, and false suspect identifications circulated before key facts were confirmed. The episode shows how synthetic media and AI summaries can intensify confusion during fast moving crises.

Fact-Checking Trump Health Rumors over Easter Weekend

Over Easter weekend, social posts falsely claimed President Trump had been hospitalized. The rumor relied on recycled video from 2024 and an AI-generated image presented as fresh evidence. The White House denied the claim, and the case became another example of how outdated footage and low-friction image generation can make false breaking-news narratives feel plausible.

How Misinformation Spread after Minnesota Lawmaker's Murder

Reporting on the Minnesota lawmaker's murder showed how incomplete early facts, speculative posts, and AI-assisted summaries combined to distort public understanding. Experts noted that the pressure to post instantly can reward confident but inaccurate claims, especially when users trust polished language or visuals more than transparent sourcing.
